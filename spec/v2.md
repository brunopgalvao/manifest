# Manifest MX v2.0 draft: the paint layer

> Paint anything, keep it readable.

v2 adds a second layer to MX. v1 nodes say **what** something is and the renderer owns the look. v2 lets a surface also say **how** something looks, down to the pixel, while every painted thing stays addressable, labelled and patchable. Every v1 document is a valid v2 document, unchanged.

Status: draft, implemented by the Rust engine (`rust/`), the TypeScript core (`packages/core`, parity-tested against fixtures the engine writes) and the DOM renderer (`packages/renderer`, a canvas is an inline SVG). Counted in `bench/results.md` §14.

## 1. Four levels of saying what to draw

A surface mixes these freely. The rule for every writer, model or importer: **use the highest level that is faithful.**

| Level | Nodes | Cost per thing on screen |
|---|---|---|
| A. semantic nodes with default paint | everything in v1 | a handful of tokens |
| B. parametrized primitives | `rect`, `circle`, `line`, `path`, `text`, `image` inside a `canvas` | ten to twenty |
| C. positioned primitives with explicit coordinates | the same, with `@x,y` | the same plus the coordinates |
| D. buffers | `series`, with `=name` bindings | once, then patches on the buffer |

Coordinates are the expensive part and models are poor at inventing them, so a `canvas` is the one place where the writer places things and everywhere else the renderer lays out.

## 2. Canvas and primitives

```
canvas WxH ["Caption"] [#id]
  rect @x,y WxH [style...] ["Label"]
  circle @cx,cy r:N [style...] ["Label"]
  line @x1,y1 @x2,y2 [style...]
  path "M 0 0 L 10 10 Z" [style...] ["Label"]
  text @x,y "String" [size:N] [weight:N] [anchor:start|middle|end] [fill:colour]
  image @x,y WxH <src> "alt"
  g [role] ["Label"] [@x,y] [WxH] [rotate:deg] [scale:k] [opacity:k]
    ...children in the group's own coordinates
```

- `canvas WxH` opens a coordinate space in CSS pixels. It is laid out like any block: at most the column's width, scaled down to fit, never up. Everything outside a canvas is v1 flow layout; everything inside is positioned.
- `@x,y` is a position, `WxH` a size, `r:N` a radius. Decimals are allowed. A `text`'s position is the start of its baseline; `anchor:` moves it.
- Style words: `fill:`, `stroke:`, `width:` (stroke width), `radius:` (corners), `opacity:`, `size:` and `weight:` (text), `anchor:`, `rotate:`, `scale:`, and the flag `dashed`. Unknown style words are errors.
- Colours are the six tones (`primary`, `success`, `danger`, `warning`, `info`, `muted`), a tone with a lightness step (`primary-70`: that fraction of the tone over the background), a palette key (`fg`, `fg2`, `bg`, `card`, `line`, `white`), a hex (`#2f5bea`, `#abc`) or `none`. Tones keep a surface consistent across the light and dark palettes; hex is the escape hatch.
- `path` takes SVG path data (`M L H V C S Q T A Z`, absolute and relative). It is the universal escape hatch and the most expensive line.
- A quoted string after the geometry is the **label**. Labels give ids (`"Leads 1,240"` → `leads`) exactly as v1 labels do, and are what perception shows. A `text`'s string is its label.
- `g` groups children under an origin and optional transform. Its box is the union of its children's, or the `WxH` it declares.

A primitive outside a canvas, or a v1 node inside one, is an error that names the line.

## 3. Roles: the same word for the thing and its role

The first word after `g` is a role from the v1 vocabulary (`button`, `link`, `field`, `select`, `check`, `toggle`, `slider`, `tab`, `metric`, `badge`, `image`, `text`, `h1`…`h3`, `table`, `row`, `col`, `card`) or a generic word (`region`, `item`, `figure`, `decoration`). Without a role word, a group is a `region`.

```
g button "Export PNG" @520,280 100x32
  rect @0,0 100x32 fill:primary radius:16
  text @50,21 "Export PNG" fill:white anchor:middle
```

- An **interactive role** (`button`, `link`, `field`, `select`, `check`, `toggle`, `slider`, `tab`) makes the group a target: it has a hit area, a click fires `action <id>` (a `link` fires `link`), and it is subject to the `!tiny` diagnostic. The host rejects an interactive group without a label.
- `decoration` is omitted from perception below level 3.
- Unknown roles are errors. The vocabulary is closed and versioned, which is what makes it readable.

## 4. Buffers: structure once, data many times

```
series sales Apr:38 May:42 Jun:51
line "Revenue" =sales
metric Latest =sales.last
text =sales.max
```

- `series <name> key:value ...` declares a named buffer. Its id is its name.
- `=name` on a chart binds the chart to the buffer. `=name.last|first|min|max|sum|count|<key>` on a `metric` or `text` binds one value.
- Patches touch the buffer, not the nodes: `+sales Jul:47` appends, `*sales.Jun 55` sets, `-sales.Apr` removes. Every bound node follows on the next paint.
- Perception shows the buffer as a summary (`series sales [6 points] min:38 max:73 last:73`) and the bindings as written (`line Revenue =sales`). `since=` reports buffer changes as `*sales.Jun 55`, `-sales.Apr` and `+sales Oct:80`.

**Instancing.** `each =buffer [row|col|N]` paints its children once per element of the buffer; inside the template `=key`, `=value` and `=index` bind to the element:

```
each =sales row
  metric =key =value
```

Every instance of a template node takes its element's key as an id suffix (`metric.Apr`), so boxes and patches address instances. Perception shows `each =sales row [6 instances]` and the template beneath it.

**Tweens.** `anim:300ms` on any node, or on a `series`, makes the renderer interpolate the next change to its numbers (buffer values, positions, sizes, radii, slider values) over that time with an ease-in-out curve instead of jumping. Perception and boxes always show the target state; only pixels move. The window and the engine page repaint at frame rate while a tween runs.

## 5. Patches

Unchanged. For painted nodes, `*id` takes any of the geometry, style and label words and replaces just those: `*won 340x48 fill:warning`, `*marker @300,180`, `*leads "Leads 1,300"`. `+funnel/ rect @200,192 160x48 fill:warning "Stalled 40"` inserts into a group; `-bg` removes. `since=` reproduces these as whole-state `*` lines.

## 6. Perception by level

| Level | A canvas shows as |
|---|---|
| 0 | counted in `nodes=` |
| 1 | `canvas 640x320 [9 shapes, 8 labelled]`, then its **labelled** shapes and groups, nested, decorations omitted. A model reading a screen full of custom paint gets a short list of things that mean something. |
| 2 | the same with `@x,y,w,h` boxes (surface-relative, after the canvas's own scaling) |
| 3 | every primitive as written, with boxes: what a model needs to patch anything it painted, and what the importer's round trip compares |

Diagnostics add `!offcanvas <id>` for a shape drawn entirely outside its canvas. `!tiny` applies to interactive groups. `look <id>` crops painted nodes like any other.

## 7. Fidelity tiers

What "paint anything" means, and how it is tested (`rust/render/tests/paint.rs`, `bench/fidelity/`):

1. **Raster passthrough.** A v1 `image` is painted at natural size, never upscaled, no rounding: a screenshot comes back pixel for pixel. 5 of 5 in the DOM renderer.
2. **Raster plus semantic overlay.** `canvas` + `image @0,0` + labelled `g` regions on top. The pixels are identical to the source (0 differing pixels on the reference photo), perception lists the regions with their roles, and an interactive region takes a click.
3. **Vector reconstruction.** A figure drawn by an independent rasterizer (PIL, 4× supersampled) reproduced with primitives: 0.62% of pixels differ by more than 32/255, mean difference 0.86/255, all at anti-aliased edges. The remaining gap is the two rasterizers' pixel conventions, stated in the MX as quarter-pixel sizes.

## 8. Importing HTML

`packages/import` turns a rendered page into MX: semantic nodes where a role fits (level A), SVG diagrams of boxes and arrows as `flow`, inline SVG as primitives (level C) and painted subtrees with no semantic reading as a `canvas` of rects and text at the page's coordinates (the demotion rule). The scorer reports reference nodes recovered, text runs kept and the round trip of the engine's boxes against the page's. Results in `bench/results.md` §13.
